# Variant: joining an established team

The team has habits, history, and relationships. Your job is to fit in, learn how they work, and earn trust before you change anything.

## What differs from core

### Listen first

- Observe how the team runs its meetings, reviews, and handoffs for at least two weeks before suggesting changes.
- Ask "why do we do it this way?" with real curiosity. Most practices have a reason.
- Keep a running list of observations. Share them as questions.

### Learn the existing system

- Ask for the team's own onboarding material, even if it is rough.
- Find the unwritten rules: who reviews what, which meetings matter, what is sensitive.
- Learn the vocabulary. Use the team's words, not ours.

### Build trust

- Deliver the small task you were given. Reliability counts more than ideas in the first weeks.
- Credit the team's work in public. Raise concerns in private first.
- Ask for help early and often. It shows you value their knowledge.

### Respect the existing roles

- Find out who owns each area. Check with the owner before you cross into it.
- Clarify how your role fits next to full-time staff. Say what you will do and what you will not.
- Learn how teammates feel about outside consultants. Past experience shapes their reaction.

### Raise change carefully

- Wait until you have context and trust.
- Pair each concern with evidence and an offer to help.
- Start with a small experiment the team agrees to, not a proposal to overhaul.

## Questions to add to week one

- What does the team do well that you hope outsiders do not break?
- What has been tried before and did not work?
- What would you like help with first?
- How did the last person who joined find their footing?
